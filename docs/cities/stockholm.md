# Stockholm

Coordinates: 59.324° N, 18.068° E. Source: [Natural Earth populated places](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-cultural-vectors/10m-populated-places/), Stockholm point, version 5.1.2.

Proportion ratios ("width ≈ 1/6 of height") are visual estimates for drawing. Heights and years are sourced.

## Viewpoint

Skeppsholmen's west shore (by the bridge to Blasieholmen), looking west across Strömmen at Gamla stan.

- Left (south): the heights of Södermalm, with Katarina kyrka's dome on the ridge.
- Middle: Gamla stan's roofs, with Tyska kyrkan's spire left of Storkyrkan's tower. Riddarholmskyrkan's iron spire rises behind them.
- Right (north): Kungliga slottet sits on the water. Stadshuset's tower stands behind it, across Riddarfjärden.
- Foreground: open water of Strömmen, then the Skeppsbron quay.

This matches the layout in `src/cities/stockholm.js`.

## Landmarks

### Stadshuset (Stockholm City Hall)
- Height: 106 m (tower). Completed: 1923 (inaugurated 23 June 1923).
- Position: right, far (behind the palace, across Riddarfjärden).
- Silhouette: long low brick block; square brick tower at the corner toward the water, width ≈ 1/7 of height; corbelled balcony (the "tornaltan", 73 m above sea level); copper belfry with open arches; small onion cap; gilded spire ending in the Three Crowns. The crown group is 8 m wide and each crown is 2.2 m in diameter. A small corner tower with a crescent moon (Måntornet) stands at the other end.
- Local cue: the three gilded crowns on the tip. They face their broad side toward the Royal Palace, so from this view they read nearly face-on.

### Riddarholmskyrkan
- Height: 90.8 m (lowest ground at the choir to the cross). The cast-iron spire alone is 42 m.
- Year: spire built during the rebuilding of 1838 to 1841, after lightning destroyed the old spire on 28 July 1835. One web summary gives 1838 to 1840. Restoration of other parts continued to 1846.
- Position: middle, far.
- Silhouette: red brick nave with steep gable; square brick tower with corner buttresses; tall openwork cast-iron spire, a lattice cone, nearly half the total height; small cross.
- Local cue: the black see-through lace spire. No other Stockholm spire is open.
- Note: the national property board (SFV) planned to dismantle the spire in late summer 2026 for restoration until 2028. The real skyline may lack the spire now.

### Tyska kyrkan (German Church)
- Height: 96 m. Highest point in Gamla stan.
- Year: old tower burned 1878. New tower by Julius Raschdorff: 1886 (Swedish Wikipedia), "by 1887" (parish history). English Wikipedia says 1878, which is the fire year.
- Position: middle-left, mid-distance.
- Silhouette: square brick tower; clock stage; short octagonal stage with small gables; very tall, slim copper needle spire, more than half the total height; rooster at the top.
- Local cue: the thinnest, tallest green needle in Gamla stan.

### Storkyrkan (Stockholm Cathedral)
- Height: sources disagree. 60.4 m to the top of the tower (English Wikipedia, after Roosval 1927). 66 m (the parish tour page and travel sites).
- Year: baroque lantern built 1736 to 1743. Restored 2020 to 2022 to its 1743 look.
- Position: middle, mid-distance (right of Tyska kyrkan).
- Silhouette: plain square tower, width ≈ 1/4 of height; cornice; balustraded terrace; tall bulb-shaped baroque lantern with open stage; small bulb; ball; short spike.
- Local cue: the bulb lantern on a square stump. Since 2022 the lantern is painted deep red, not copper green.

### Kungliga slottet (Royal Palace)
- Height: not found in sources. Wings are 48 m long (except the south-west wing, 11 m).
- Year: ready for the royal family on 7 December 1754.
- Position: right, near, on the water.
- Silhouette: one big flat-topped block; slightly raised corner pavilions; flat roofline hidden behind a balustrade; no towers or domes. Many even rows of windows.
- Local cue: the huge flat box with no roof showing.

### Katarina kyrka
- Height: 72 m to the top (tower designed by Göran Josuæ Adelcrantz after the 1723 fire).
- Year: inaugurated 1695. Burned 1723 and on 17 May 1990. Re-inaugurated 1995 with the pre-1723 tower form.
- Position: far left, far, high on the Södermalm ridge.
- Silhouette: cross-shaped church with gables; central octagonal drum; tall dome; lantern; short spire. Four small corner towers surround the central tower.
- Local cue: the big dome high on Söder with four small turrets around it.

### Globen (Avicii Arena)
- Height: 85.20 m from the ground. External diameter: 110.40 m.
- Year: inaugurated 19 February 1989.
- Position: far left, south beyond Söder at Johanneshov. From Skeppsholmen it hides behind Söder; the scene lowers Söder's east end so it shows, as most drawn Stockholm skylines do.
- Silhouette: white sphere with a visible steel grid, on a low arena base.
- Local cue: the white ball itself.

### Kaknästornet
- Height: 155 m; about 170 m with the mast. 34 floors.
- Year: built from 1963, inaugurated 12 May 1967. Architects Hans Borgström and Bengt Lindroos.
- Position: right, far. It stands on Gärdet, north-east of Skeppsholmen, so from this viewpoint it is behind the viewer. The scene puts it north of Stadshuset, as drawn Stockholm skylines include it.
- Silhouette: tall square concrete shaft, width ≈ 1/12 of the height to the roof; the head with the restaurant, café and antenna platforms is turned 45 degrees to the shaft, so it reads wider; lattice mast on the roof.
- Local cue: the pale needle with the thick head, the tallest thing on the skyline.

## Traits

- snow: true. Snow falls and settles in Stockholm winters.
- adventStars: true. Lit paper stars in windows are a Swedish December custom.
- ice: true. Riddarfjärden froze over completely in the winter of 2023/24, and
  floating ice floes have been reported in central Stockholm. Strömmen carries
  Mälaren's outflow, so the scene shows floes and ice along the quay, and wider
  cover only with a lot of snow.

## Filler

Gamla stan: narrow 17th and 18th century merchant houses, tall and thin. Roofs are steep, red or black tin or tile, many with stepped or curved gables facing the water. Facades are ochre, rust red, salmon, cream and pale yellow plaster. Skeppsbron is one continuous wall of these facades. Södermalm behind: a dark ridge with houses stepping up the slope.
