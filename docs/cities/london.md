# London

Coordinates: 51.502° N, 0.119° W. Source: [Natural Earth populated places](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-cultural-vectors/10m-populated-places/), London point, version 5.1.2.

Proportion ratios are visual estimates for drawing. Heights and years are sourced. Feet are converted at 1 ft = 0.3048 m.

## Viewpoint

Waterloo Bridge, looking east (downstream) along the Thames.

- Left: the north bank (Victoria Embankment), with St Paul's dome above the roofs.
- Middle, far: the City cluster of towers, behind and right of St Paul's.
- Right: The Shard on the south bank, far taller than its neighbours. The Oxo Tower stands near the water in front.
- Foreground: the wide river with bridges crossing in the distance.

Alternative card (same bridge, looking west): the Houses of Parliament with Elizabeth Tower (Big Ben), 316 ft (96 m), completed 31 May 1859; and the London Eye, 135 m high, 120 m in diameter, completed March 2000.

## Landmarks

### St Paul's Cathedral
- Height: 365 ft (111 m) to the cross.
- Completed: 1710.
- Position: left, mid-distance.
- Silhouette: wide baroque church; two west towers with small open lanterns; a tall drum ringed by columns; a balustrade; a high lead dome, taller than a half sphere; stone lantern; gilded ball and cross.
- Local cue: the grey dome. Everyone in London knows it.

### The Shard
- Height: 309.6 m.
- Completed: 5 July 2012.
- Position: right, mid-distance, south bank.
- Silhouette: a slim glass pyramid; the faces do not meet at the top, so the tip breaks into several splinters; width at the base ≈ 1/5 of height.
- Local cue: the broken glass tip.

### 22 Bishopsgate
- Height: 278 m (roof).
- Completed: 2020.
- Position: middle, far, the tallest block in the City cluster.
- Silhouette: a plain, broad glass block with slightly faceted sides and a flat top.
- Local cue: the big flat-topped slab behind the others.

### 122 Leadenhall Street ("the Cheesegrater")
- Height: 225 m (roof).
- Completed: June 2013; opened July 2014.
- Position: middle, far, next to 22 Bishopsgate.
- Silhouette: a tall right-angled wedge; one side vertical, the other a straight slope from the top down to the base.
- Local cue: the sloped wedge.

### 30 St Mary Axe ("the Gherkin")
- Height: 180 m (roof).
- Completed: 2003; opened 28 April 2004.
- Position: middle, far, left of the Cheesegrater.
- Silhouette: a bullet shape; widest a little below the middle, curving in to a rounded tip; diagonal stripes of dark glass.
- Local cue: the rounded bullet.

### 20 Fenchurch Street ("the Walkie-Talkie")
- Height: 160 m (roof).
- Completed: April 2014.
- Position: middle-right, far, a little nearer than the others.
- Silhouette: a block that widens toward the top; the top is curved and slightly slanted, like a phone handset.
- Local cue: top-heavy, wider at the top than at the base.

## Traits

- snow: false. Snow falls on an average of 16 days a year in London, but it is
  infrequently heavy. No source found gives days with lying snow in central
  London, so the scene does not show settled snow.
- adventStars: false. Lit paper stars in windows are a German and Swedish
  custom. No source found names it as a London custom.
- ice: false. The last frost fair on the Thames was in 1814. After old London
  Bridge was replaced and the river was embanked, the river became less likely
  to freeze.

## Rights

- Rule: Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988, section 62. It applies to all
  buildings, and to sculptures and works of artistic craftsmanship that are
  permanently in a public place. A graphic work, photograph or film of them
  does not infringe copyright. Issuing copies to the public does not infringe
  either. The section has no non-commercial limit.
- All six landmarks are buildings. The London Eye on the alternative card is
  permanently in a public place, so section 62(1)(b) covers it if it is not a
  building.
- Night: section 62 has no rule for lighting. The drawn view may show the
  landmarks lit at night.
- Leave out: nothing found.

## Filler

The north bank: Victorian and Edwardian stone office blocks with mansard roofs, small domes and pediments. Colours are Portland stone white, grey and buff. The south bank: brick warehouses and post-war concrete blocks, lower and flat-roofed.
